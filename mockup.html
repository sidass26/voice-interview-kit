5 Days in Muscat: A Product Manager's Honest Guide to Oman's Surprising Capital

✍️ Priya Rajan 📍 Muscat, Oman 🕐 8 min read Published May 10, 2025
PR
Priya Rajan
Senior Product Manager
I work in product, which means I spend a lot of time thinking about what makes travel experiences genuinely good. I try to travel somewhere new every two months — usually solo, always on a budget.
𝕏 @priya_travels in LinkedIn ✉ priya@company.com
Published: May 10, 2025
Trip taken: January 2025
Last updated: May 10, 2025
✓ First-hand experience

I almost didn't go to Oman. I had Sri Lanka booked, the flights confirmed, the guesthouses saved — and then a layover in Muscat during a conference two years ago changed everything. I went back, properly, this January.

Nobody in my friend group had been to Oman. The common response when I told people I was going alone was "Oh, is that safe?" followed by "Where exactly is that?" which is, honestly, part of why I fell for it. Five days. Solo. Mid-January, which I now know is the perfect time — 22°C, clear skies, zero tourists at the major sites.

"I expected sand dunes and heat. What I got was Michelin-worthy Omani food, zero tourists, and the most hospitable people I've ever encountered anywhere in the world."

Day 1–2: Muscat — More European than I Expected

The first thing that hit me landing at Muscat International was how clean it was. Not "airport clean" — genuinely, the whole city is like that. White buildings, strict signage laws (no billboards), mountains on one side, the Gulf on the other. It looked like a city designed by someone who'd seen too many beautiful Mediterranean towns and decided to try.

🕌 Grand Mosque interior — Muscat, Day 1
📍 Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque, Muscat — "Arrive at 8am when it opens. The carpet is the size of a football pitch and the chandelier alone has 600,000 Swarovski crystals."

The Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque is unavoidable and should be. Get there at opening (8am). Dress code is strict — full coverage, head scarf for women — but they have wraps available at the entrance if you forget. The interior is staggering: a handwoven carpet that took 600 women four years to complete, a chandelier with 600,000 crystals. Worth every minute of the early alarm.

Day 3: Nizwa Fort + The Souk Nobody Tells You About

I rented a car for this day — 100% recommend. The Nizwa drive is 1.5 hours from Muscat and goes through the Hajar Mountains, which look like Mars. Nizwa Fort is genuinely impressive (not tourist-trap impressive, actually historically interesting), but the real find is the Nizwa Souk right next to it.

🏔️ Hajar Mountains en route to Nizwa
📍 Hajar Mountains, Al Dakhiliyah — "Stop anywhere on this drive. Every vista is better than the last."

Food: What I Actually Ate (and What You Should)

Omani food was the biggest surprise of the trip. I expected generic Gulf fare — shawarma, hummus, rice. What I found was a cuisine with serious Persian and East African influence that produces results like shuwa (slow-cooked lamb, 48 hours, underground clay oven) and harees (slow-cooked wheat and meat porridge that sounds awful and tastes extraordinary).
